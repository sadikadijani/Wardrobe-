# DIY Wardrobe Planner

A light, child-safe, free-standing wardrobe that a beginner can build with a drill, a handsaw and Hornbach's cutting service. No wall or ceiling fixing needed.

Open **`index.html`** in a browser for the interactive 3D planner. Change the width, height, depth, legs, box height, doors and stability options, and the cut list, shopping list and build steps update to match. You can switch on GitHub Pages for this repo to use it from your phone.

## The chosen design (planner defaults)

- **Size:** 120 cm wide × 60 cm deep × 180 cm tall
- **Body:** 18 mm poplar plywood (Pappelsperrholz). It's the lightest common board that still holds screws well.
- **Layout:** one open hanging space with a single rail (centre support included), and two open boxes below for shoes and folded clothes
- **Base:** a frame of 44 × 44 mm spruce battens on 15 cm legs, so a robot vacuum fits underneath
- **Doors:** two doors made of a plywood frame with frosted twin-wall polycarbonate windows. About 3.5 kg each, on normal hidden cup hinges.
- **Back:** 3 mm hardboard, which keeps the box square
- **Weight:** about 50 kg without the ballast, and every piece is light enough to carry alone
- **Assembly:** steel angle brackets and screws only (no glue), so it comes apart for moving

## How it stays child-safe without the wall

The only essential changes are two cheap ones:

1. **Anti-tip foot:** the base frame sticks out 10 cm at the front, under the doors. This moves the tipping edge forward. Cost: three slightly longer battens.
2. **Hidden ballast:** 2 concrete paving slabs (40 × 40 × 4 cm, about 14.5 kg each, a few euros apiece) lie at the back of the boxes under a loose plywood false floor. They come out when you move.

The planner checks a 27 kg child (the 60 lb test weight in the US furniture tip-over standard ASTM F2057-23) in two situations. First, hanging from the top front edge and leaning back, which pulls outward with half their weight. Second, standing on the edge of an open door. It checks both with the wardrobe **empty**, which is the worst case, and adds a 15 % safety margin. With the defaults, an empty wardrobe needs about an **18 kg** pull at shoulder height to tip, and about **25 kg** with a normal load. Without the foot and ballast it would tip at about 7 kg.

Without the foot, it would need 5 slabs instead of 2. That's why the foot matters most.

> This is an engineering estimate, not a certified test. Once it's built, check it empty with a luggage scale: pull the top front edge forward at shoulder height while someone stands behind to catch it. The back legs shouldn't lift below about 18 kg. If they do, add a slab. Fixing it to the wall or bracing it against the ceiling (both options are in the planner) is still the most secure choice.

## Shopping list

- **Doors:** Two framed doors: 18 mm poplar blanks with the windows cut out by Hornbach, and 6 mm opal twin-wall polycarbonate (Stegplatte opal) behind the windows. About 3 kg per door. Fix each insert with thin wood strips and 3 × 12 mm screws, or mounting tape plus a few screws. 8 × 35 mm cup hinges, full overlay (Topfband aufliegend), with mounting plates. Soft-close is worth the few extra euros. Plus 2 small knobs. Ask Hornbach to drill the hinge cups (Topfbandbohrung), 4 per door, the end ones 10 cm from the top and bottom.
- **Panels:** 18 mm poplar plywood (Pappelsperrholz). Roughly 2 full sheets of 2500 × 1250 mm (typical size, ask in store). The cuts use about 4.3 m².
- **Back:** 1 × 3 mm hardboard (HDF), cut to 1200 × 1606 mm
- **Battens:** Planed spruce 44 × 44 mm, about 4.9 m in total plus one shelf stiffener
- **Legs:** 6 × furniture legs 15 cm, adjustable, with screw-on mounting plates (Möbelfüße mit Montageplatte)
- **Brackets:** about 30 × steel angle brackets ~30 × 30 mm (Winkelverbinder)
- **Screws:** 4 × 16 mm chipboard screws (box of 200), 4 × 40 mm (box of 50) to fix the box to the frame, 3 × 16 mm for the back
- **Hanging:** 1 × oval wardrobe rail (Kleiderstange oval) + 2 end holders, cut to 116 cm with a small hacksaw. Add 1 centre rail support (Mittelträger) screwed under the top, because the span is over 1 m.
- **Ballast:** 2 × concrete paving slabs 40 × 40 × 4 cm (Gehwegplatte / Betonplatte, about 14.5 kg each, a few euros apiece). Check the weight on the label: if yours are lighter, buy one more. They lie at the back of the boxes under a loose 12 mm plywood false floor, and come out when you move. Stick felt pads under them.
- **False floor:** Spacer strips to hold the false floor 5 cm up: 4 short batten offcuts per box
- **Anti-tip foot:** Nothing extra: the base frame is 10 cm longer at the front.
- **Stability:** 2 × anti-slip pads for the front legs, so the wardrobe can't slide forward.
- **Tools you still need:** 2.5 and 3 mm wood drill bits, countersink bit, PH2 screwdriver bits, tape measure, carpenter's square, 2 clamps, spirit level, small hacksaw for the rail, luggage scale for the final test.

## Build steps

1. Send the cut list to Hornbach (online “Holzzuschnitt” or at the cutting desk). For the doors, show them the window note under the cut list. Mark each piece with painter's tape when you collect it.
2. Build the base frame on the floor: two long battens and three cross battens, two angle brackets per joint. The cross battens are 61 cm long, so the frame sticks out 10 cm in front of the wardrobe. That's the anti-tip foot. Round its top front edge with sandpaper. Screw the leg plates underneath at the corners and under the middle cross batten.
3. Lay one side panel flat. Stand the top and bottom on its edge and join each with 2–3 angle brackets on the inside. Pre-drill 2.5 mm holes and keep the pieces flush with a square.
4. Add the second side the same way. Fix the short box divider upright on the bottom panel at 57 cm from the left side. It also holds up the middle of the shelf.
5. With the box lying face-down, check it is square (both diagonals equal), then screw the hardboard back on every ~20 cm with 3 × 16 mm screws. This is what makes it rigid.
6. Stand the box up on the base frame, flush with the back edge (two people). Screw down through the bottom panel into the battens with 4 × 40 mm screws, about 6 in total.
7. Put the shelf in at 40 cm above the bottom, resting on brackets at the sides and on the box divider. Screw the stiffener batten under its front edge so it doesn't sag. Mount the rail ~5 cm under the top.
8. Stand it in place and level it with the adjustable legs, then screw the front legs out 2–3 mm more than the back ones so it leans very slightly back. Lay the 2 concrete slabs flat at the back of the boxes, put the spacer strips around them and drop the false floor on top.
9. Doors: lay each door face-down, place the polycarbonate inserts over the windows (flutes vertical), and hold them with thin strips screwed into the frame. Screw the hinges into the pre-drilled cups, clip them onto the plates on the sides, and adjust the gaps with the hinge screws.
10. Test it once, with the wardrobe empty: stand behind it to catch it, hook a luggage scale to a strap around the top front edge, and pull forward slowly. It should reach about 18 kg before the back legs lift. If it lifts earlier, add a slab.
